## What Are First Principles?

First principles are the foundational propositions that cannot be deduced from any other proposition within the system. They are the axioms upon which LCDD is built. If you disagree with a first principle, you disagree with LCDD at its root. If you agree with the first principles but disagree with a specific specification document, that is a refinement discussion.

---

## The Five Axioms

### Axiom 1: All software is governed by constraints, whether acknowledged or not.

Every software system operates under a set of constraints — explicit rules encoded in tests, implicit assumptions embedded in architecture, external regulations that apply by law, team conventions that govern code style, and emergent patterns that define what "normal" looks like. There is no such thing as ungoverned software; there is only software whose governors are invisible.

**Corollary:** Making constraints visible is always better than leaving them implicit.

### Axiom 2: In the age of AI-assisted development, constraint governance is the bottleneck, not code production.

The limiting factor in software development has shifted from "how fast can we write code?" to "how do we ensure the code that is written — by humans and AI agents alike — respects the constraints that matter?" Code generation speed increases risk when governance does not scale proportionally.

**Corollary:** Investments in constraint governance yield higher returns than investments in code generation speed, once a baseline of AI-assisted productivity is achieved.

### Axiom 3: Constraints exist on a spectrum of authority, from individual preference to legal mandate.

Not all constraints are equal. A team member's preferred variable naming convention and a government regulation carrying criminal liability for non-compliance are both "constraints," but they demand fundamentally different governance — different review processes, different enforcement mechanisms, different observability. A unified methodology must accommodate the full spectrum.

**Corollary:** A constraint's governance cost should be proportional to its authority and blast radius.

### Axiom 4: The sources of constraints are heterogeneous and evolving; a methodology that cannot ingest from arbitrary sources is incomplete.

Constraints originate from PDFs published by government agencies, Markdown files in repositories, decisions made in video calls, emergent patterns in production data, and suggestions generated by AI. A methodology that only handles constraints authored in a specific format or stored in a specific location cannot claim to govern software — it governs only the subset of constraints convenient enough to encode.

**Corollary:** The ingestion pipeline must be source-agnostic. The schema is the universal adapter.

### Axiom 5: Observability without enforcement is documentation; enforcement without observability is dogma.

A constraint that is observed but not enforced is a suggestion. A constraint that is enforced but not observed is a blind rule — you cannot know if it is effective, if it is being violated in production, or if it has become obsolete. The feedback loop between enforcement and observation is what makes a constraint *living*.

**Corollary:** Every constraint in the Active stage must have both an enforcement mechanism and an observability mechanism, or its Active status is invalid.

## Derived Principles

From these five axioms, we derive the operational principles that guide specification design:

| Principle | Derived From |
|---|---|
| Contexts must be versioned immutably | Axiom 1 (visible constraints require traceable history) |
| Contexts must have explicit lifecycle stage | Axiom 2 (governance requires knowing what's active) |
| Authority model must support graduated trust levels | Axiom 3 (spectrum of authority) |
| Schema must be source-agnostic | Axiom 4 (heterogeneous sources) |
| Every Active context must link to both enforcement and observability | Axiom 5 (living constraint) |
| Conflict between contexts must be surfaced, not resolved silently | Axiom 3 (equal authority can conflict) |
| Contexts are queryable by authority, source, domain, and lifecycle stage | Axiom 2 (governance requires retrieval) |

## Non-Axioms (What We Explicitly Do Not Assume)

- **We do not assume AI agents are trustworthy.** LCDD is designed for the scenario where AI agents may make mistakes, take shortcuts, or optimize for the wrong objective. Constraints exist *because* agents are fallible.
- **We do not assume constraints are static.** The methodology explicitly plans for evolving constraints. A frozen rulebook is a liability.
- **We do not assume a single source of truth.** Constraints from different authorities may conflict. LCDD provides mechanisms for conflict management, not conflict elimination.
- **We do not assume a specific technology stack.** The methodology is abstract enough to be implemented in any language and enforced through any mechanism.
- **We do not assume organizational scale.** LCDD works for a solo founder and for a 1,000-person engineering organization. The governance mechanisms scale.
