# 0003 — Authority Model

**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Specification:** Living Context Driven Development  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-06

---

## Abstract

This document defines the Authority Model — how trust is established, delegated, challenged, and revoked for Contexts. The authority model answers the question: "Who says this is a rule, and why should we trust them?"

---

## Motivation

Not all constraints are equal. A PCI-DSS requirement, a CTO's architectural directive, a team member's code style preference, and an AI's autogenerated suggestion all assert constraints — but they demand fundamentally different levels of trust, scrutiny, and enforcement. Without an explicit authority model, all constraints are treated equally, leading either to excessive governance overhead (reviewing every preference change like a security requirement) or dangerous under-governance (treating regulatory mandates like style suggestions). See [0000-problem.md] P6.

---

## The Authority Spectrum

### Authority Levels

| Level | Name | Definition | Example | Default Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Mandate | Legal, regulatory, or contractual obligation. Violation carries external consequences. | OJK POJK regulation, PCI-DSS, GDPR | Block |
| 3 | Standard | Organization-wide policy established by a central authority. Internal consequences for violation. | CISO security standard, CTO architecture patterns | Block |
| 2 | Guideline | Team-level or domain-level best practice. Violation should be justified. | Recommended libraries, code style conventions | Warn |
| 1 | Preference | Individual or small-team convention. Informational only. | Editor settings, naming taste | Comment |
| 0 | Suggestion | AI-generated or community-suggested constraint. Untrusted by default. | LLM-extracted constraint, community Context Pack | Comment or Silent |

### Authority Level Inheritance

Authority level flows through delegation. When a CTO (level 3) delegates to a Tech Lead (level 3 for delegated scope, level 2 for general), the Tech Lead's contexts carry level 2 authority unless explicitly elevated through the delegation chain.

## The Authority Object

Every Context MUST contain an `authority` block:

```yaml
authority:
  source:
    type: "individual" | "organization" | "standard-body" | "ai-system" | "community" | "automated"
    id: "ciso-office"                    # Unique identifier for the source
    name: "CISO Office, PT Example"     # Human-readable name
    uri: "https://security.example.com" # Optional: source URL

  level: 3                              # 0-4 per the authority spectrum

  delegation:
    - from: "cto-office"
      to: "ciso-office"
      scope: "security"
      effective_date: "2025-01-01"
      expiration: null                  # null = indefinite
    - from: "ciso-office"
      to: "appsec-team"
      scope: "application-security"
      effective_date: "2025-06-01"
      expiration: "2026-06-01"

  trust_model: "direct" | "delegated" | "community-consensus" | "ai-inferred"
  trust_score: 0.95                     # Optional: 0.0 to 1.0, calculated from evidence
  challenge_policy:
    process: "file-issue"
    uri: "https://github.com/org/contexts/issues/new"
    sla_hours: 72
```

## Authority Sources

### Individual

A specific person asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- Architecture Decision Records authored by a Tech Lead.
- Coding standards defined by a team member.
- Domain expertise from a subject-matter expert.

**Trust Basis:** Individual's role, expertise, and organizational authority.

### Organization

A department, team, or committee asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- Security standards from the CISO office.
- Platform requirements from the infrastructure team.
- Quality gates from the QA team.

**Trust Basis:** Organizational charter, cross-team consensus, management chain.

### Standard Body

An external standards organization asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- PCI-DSS, ISO 27001, SOC 2 requirements.
- W3C, IETF, or ECMA standards.
- Government regulatory agencies (OJK, Kemenkop, etc.).

**Trust Basis:** External authority recognized by the organization's compliance framework.

### AI System

An LLM, ML model, or automated system asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- LLM-extracted constraints from regulatory documents.
- Pattern-based suggestions from codebase analysis.
- Fitness-function-generated constraint variants.

**Trust Basis:** Statistical confidence, human review status, track record. AI-inferred contexts start at level 0 and graduate with human review.

### Community

A community-maintained Context Pack asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- OWASP Top 10 security pack.
- Startup best practices pack.
- Industry-specific regulatory packs.

**Trust Basis:** Community reputation, review count, adoption metrics, maintainer track record.

### Automated

A deterministic system (linter, static analyzer, contract test) asserts the constraint. Typically used for:
- Type system invariants.
- Lint rules with zero false positives.
- Contract test specifications.

**Trust Basis:** Determinism, formal verification, zero false positive rate over observation period.

## Authority Challenge and Revocation

### Challenge Process

Any stakeholder MAY challenge an Active context's authority. The challenge process depends on authority level:

| Level | Challenge Method | Resolution Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 4 (Mandate) | Formal compliance review | Legal/compliance team + external counsel |
| 3 (Standard) | Issue filed in governance system | Authority owner + one level up in delegation chain |
| 2 (Guideline) | PR or issue in team repository | Team lead |
| 1 (Preference) | Comment or discussion thread | Individual or team consensus |
| 0 (Suggestion) | Downvote or dismiss | No formal process; auto-archived if challenged multiple times |

### Challenge Lifecycle

```
Challenge Filed → Review → Resolution → (Upheld | Revised | Revoked)
```

A challenge MUST:
1. Identify the context being challenged.
2. State the reason (incorrect, outdated, overly broad, conflicting, authority level wrong).
3. Provide evidence (counter-example, updated regulation, production data).
4. Be recorded as a lifecycle event on the challenged context.

### Revocation

When authority is revoked:
1. The context transitions to Deprecated with the revocation as the deprecation reason.
2. If the context was at level 4 (Mandate), legal/compliance MUST sign off on revocation.
3. A replacement context MAY be created at the appropriate authority level.
4. The revocation decision is permanently recorded in the context's lifecycle events.

## Trust Model

### Direct Trust

The authority source is the original definer of the constraint. Trust flows directly from the source to the context.

**Example:** A CISO writes "All production secrets must be stored in Vault" directly as a Context. The CISO's authority backs the context directly.

### Delegated Trust

The authority source has delegated authority to a sub-entity. Trust flows through the delegation chain.

**Example:** The CTO delegates security policy authority to the CISO, who delegates application security authority to the AppSec team. A context authored by the AppSec team carries delegated trust through the chain CTO → CISO → AppSec.

### Community Consensus Trust

Authority derives from community adoption and review. Trust is proportional to usage, review count, and maintainer reputation.

**Example:** A community Context Pack for "OWASP Top 10 for Node.js" carries trust because it is widely used, peer-reviewed, and maintained by recognized security practitioners.

### AI-Inferred Trust

Authority derives from an AI system's confidence score, which MUST be validated by human review before the context reaches level 2 or above.

**Example:** An LLM extracts "User passwords must be hashed with bcrypt (cost >= 12)" from an internal security document. The context starts at level 0 (Suggestion). After human review confirms correctness, it graduates to level 3 (Standard).

---

## Trust Score (Optional, Non-Normative)

Implementations MAY calculate a trust score (0.0 to 1.0) for each context based on:

| Factor | Weight Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Authority level (0-4, normalized) | 30% |
| Human review count | 15% |
| Violation challenge success rate (challenges upheld vs. total challenges) | 20% |
| Age (older = higher, with decay for unreviewed) | 10% |
| Observability data (false positive rate) | 15% |
| Community reputation (for Community sources) | 10% |

Trust score is advisory, not normative. It MAY be used for sorting, filtering, or dashboards but MUST NOT be used as the sole determinant of enforcement behavior.

## Delegation Rules

### Scope Limitation

Delegation MUST specify a scope. A CISO delegating "security policy" to the AppSec team does NOT delegate "physical security" or "compliance reporting" unless those scopes are explicitly included.

### Temporal Limitation

Delegation MAY have an expiration date. Expired delegations revert authority to the delegator. Contexts created under an expired delegation remain valid but cannot be modified without re-delegation or escalation.

### Revocability

Delegation is revocable by the delegator at any time. Revocation does NOT automatically deprecate contexts created under the delegation — those contexts remain Active until explicitly deprecated through the standard lifecycle.

### Chain Integrity

The delegation chain MUST be complete and verifiable. A context whose delegation chain has a broken link (delegator no longer exists, delegation expired without renewal) MUST be flagged for review. It does NOT automatically lose authority — that would be a denial-of-service vector — but it MUST be surfaced for human judgment.

## Conflict Resolution by Authority

When two Active contexts conflict, authority level determines the resolution path:

| Authority Levels | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Same level | Human decision required; conflict surfaced as event |
| One higher | Higher authority context takes precedence; lower context flagged for review |
| Level 4 vs. Level 4 | Emergency escalation to legal/compliance; both contexts remain Active with conflict surfaced |
| Level 0 vs. any | Level 0 is suppressed; no conflict event |

However, precedence does NOT mean the lower-authority context is wrong. It means it is temporarily overridden. The conflict is still recorded. This is critical: suppression is not resolution.
